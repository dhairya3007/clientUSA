MERENGUE

UNDERSTANDING MERENGUE

Danced in a Continuous Rhythm

Merengue is a partner dance built around a simple idea: continuous movement and shared rhythm.

The basic movement is compact and grounded. Rather than relying on large travelling patterns, the dancers shift their weight from one foot to the other while maintaining a steady connection with the music and with one another.

The steps are simple to understand.
The movement comes from what happens between them.

WHERE IT COMES FROM

From the Dominican Republic

Merengue originated in the Dominican Republic and became one of the country's defining musical and dance traditions.

Its development brought together influences from European, African and Caribbean traditions, creating a rhythm that is distinctly Dominican.

Over time, Merengue became deeply embedded in social life — danced at gatherings, celebrations and across generations.

Its music is energetic and unmistakable.
Its dance is equally direct.
Two people, one rhythm, and a continuous exchange of movement.

HOW IT WORKS

The Walking Motion

At the centre of Merengue is a remarkably simple weight transfer. Imagine walking without actually travelling forward.

01

SHIFT

The dancer transfers their weight onto one foot.

02

SHIFT

The weight moves naturally onto the other.

03

CONTINUE

The movement continues from side to side, maintaining the rhythm.

04

CONNECT

The partner responds to the same rhythm, allowing the two movements to become one continuous exchange.

The feet create the foundation.
The body follows the rhythm.
The connection between the dancers determines how the movement develops.

From this simple action, turns, direction changes, hand patterns and more elaborate combinations can be introduced.

THE STRUCTURE OF THE DANCE

Simple Movement. Continuous Rhythm.

Merengue does not depend on complicated footwork to create character. Its strength comes from consistency, rhythm and connection.

Partners can remain almost in place or travel through the space while maintaining the same underlying movement.

The Lead communicates direction and intention.
The Follow responds through movement and timing.
The rhythm keeps everything connected.

Because the basic step is so accessible, attention can move beyond the feet — towards musicality, posture, body movement and the quality of the connection between two people.

The structure remains simple.
The expression can become anything but simple.
The rhythm provides the foundation.
The connection gives it character.
